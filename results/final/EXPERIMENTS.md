## 1. Setup

**Data.** We use the stored Llama-3-8B-Instruct samples from the Monkey Business release: 127 GSM8K questions
and 128 MATH questions, each with 10,000 sampled solutions (`data/processed/<CONFIG>.npz`). Samples are grouped
into answer clusters: cluster 0 is every sample the dataset labels correct, and wrong samples are grouped by their
normalized final-answer string. Mean pass@1 is 0.768 on GSM8K and 0.268 on MATH
(`results/final/judge_analysis/per_question.csv`, column `pass_at_1`).

**Judge.** Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct judges a representative solution of each question's three most common answers,
8 verdicts at temperature 0.7 per (question, answer) (`data/processed/<CONFIG>_judge.csv`). Pooled false-pass
(wrong answer judged CORRECT) and false-fail (correct answer judged INCORRECT) rates are 0.260 / 0.548 on GSM8K
and 0.139 / 0.736 on MATH (`results/final/<CONFIG>/lam2_jr2/meta.json`). In simulation, a JUDGE call on answer
*k* of question *q* passes with that answer's empirical pass rate. Answers outside the top 3 use the pooled rates.

**Decision problem.** One episode is one question. The agent gets one free attempt, then at most H = 20 checks
(RESAMPLE costs c_s, JUDGE costs c_j), and ends with SUBMIT (+1 if correct, −λ if wrong) or ABSTAIN (0).
We fix c_s = 0.01 and vary λ ∈ {1.2, 2, 5} at c_j/c_s = 2, and c_j/c_s ∈ {0.5, 1, 2} at λ = 2, on both datasets
(10 runs). Compute is reported in resample-equivalents: #resamples + (c_j/c_s)·#judges.

**Cross-validation.** 5-fold CV over questions with the same fold split as the earlier runs (seed 0; 101–102
training and 25–26 test questions per fold). Everything that is fit or tuned uses only the training questions of
the fold. That covers the DP belief model (P(leader correct | n, a, b), vote transitions, and the judge's Beta
parameters) and every baseline parameter. Each question is a test question exactly once, so every policy gets one
held-out mean reward per question.

**Episodes and seeds.**
* Belief model: 400 simulated 21-sample episodes per training question, seed 0 + fold (unchanged from `policies/dp.py`).
* Baseline tuning: 100 episodes per training question on RNG stream *tune*.
* Test: 100 episodes per test question on RNG stream *test*.

Episode (q, e) of a stream gets its own generator seeded by (seed, stream, fold, q, e). So every policy sees the
same sample order in test episode (q, e) (common random numbers), and tuning and test draws never overlap. Only
the judge verdict draws differ between policies that call the judge. Episode counts were not reduced: a full run
takes under 20 s on 5 processes (`meta.json`, `runtime_s`).

**Fast baseline evaluation.** Resample-only rules are scored from the 21-sample trajectories, which gives
results identical to stepping `sim/env.py` episode by episode (`tests/test_final.py::test_fast_rules_match_env_exactly`).

## 2. Baselines and tuning protocol

* **first answer**: submit the first sample.
* **fixed-k (tuned)**: majority vote over k samples, k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 13, 17, 21}.
* **margin stop (tuned)**: resample until the leader leads the runner-up by m votes (or 21 attempts), m ∈ {1..8}.
* **margin stop+abstain (tuned)**: as above, but abstain if the margin is not reached after k_max attempts;
  (m, k_max) ∈ {2..8} × {5, 10, 15, 20}.

For each fold, every grid value is simulated on the fold's training questions, and the value with the highest
mean reward under the run's costs is chosen. Ties go to the cheaper value. The chosen value is then evaluated on
the test questions. Baselines never call the judge, so their choice does not depend on c_j. We also report each
rule with the single value that maximizes pooled **test** reward, labeled **"oracle-tuned on test"**. This is the
(unfair) protocol of the earlier results, kept only for reference.

## 4. Statistical significance

For each pair we take the per-question difference in mean test reward (policy − baseline, n = 127 or 128 questions)
and report:
* the mean difference with a 95% percentile bootstrap CI over questions (10,000 resamples);
* the median difference;
* a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank p-value, Holm-adjusted within each table;
* the share of questions where the policy is better or worse.

The two tests answer different questions. The CI is about the **mean** reward, which is the objective. The
Wilcoxon test is about whether the differences are centered at 0. Rewards differ in a skewed way: an abstaining
policy pays a small cost on many easy questions and gains a lot on a few hard ones. The two tests therefore
sometimes disagree. We call a difference significant ("yes") only when the CI excludes 0 **and** the Holm p < 0.05,
and we flag the cases where only one of them rejects.

**Reading the λ = 2 tables.**
* **GSM8K: the DP does not beat a well-tuned margin rule.**
  * With abstaining, DP (resample, abstain) and margin stop+abstain (tuned) are tied: +0.004 [−0.013, +0.022],
    Wilcoxon p = 0.94, better on 46% and worse on 46% of questions.
  * Without abstaining, DP (resample) is slightly but significantly *worse* than tuned margin stop:
    −0.009 [−0.017, −0.002], worse on 76% of questions. It also uses more compute (4.64 vs 3.32 resample-equivalents).
  * Both DPs beat tuned fixed-k by about +0.05 reward per question (+0.050 [+0.029, +0.074]).
* **MATH: the DP with abstaining beats the tuned abstaining rule, by a small margin.**
  * DP (resample, abstain) vs margin stop+abstain (tuned): +0.023 [+0.012, +0.034], Holm p = 1.4e-6, better on
    76% of questions.
  * Both abstain about 82% of the time and use the same compute (3.80 vs 3.75 resample-equivalents).
  * The earlier "about 2× the reward at half the compute" compared against a rule with k_max fixed at 10 and m
    picked on test (best such rule: reward 0.030, `results/final/MATH_Llama-3-8B-Instruct/lam2_jr2/per_question.csv`).
    Once k_max is tuned on training questions, the compute advantage disappears. The reward gap (0.061 vs 0.038)
    is real but small in absolute terms.
* **Abstaining matters far more than the stopping rule.**
  * On MATH, letting the DP abstain adds +1.009 [+0.825, +1.195] reward per question.
  * On GSM8K it adds +0.068 [+0.008, +0.135] to the mean, but the median difference is −0.001 and Holm p = 0.07.
    The gain comes from a minority of hard questions (better on 17%, worse on 65%).

## 5. Calibration of the belief model

At every state of every test trajectory (n = 1..21, 100 episodes per test question, model fit on the other folds)
we log the vote-only belief pc[n, a, b] and whether the leader is correct. We also log:
* the posterior P(correct | n, a, b, jp, jf) at every decision state of DP (+judge, abstain);
* a **probe**: 1–3 simulated judge verdicts on the leader at every trajectory state, so that the
  posterior-after-judge is evaluated on many states, not just the few where the DP chose to judge.

ECE uses 10 equal-width bins. Reliability diagrams with per-bin counts: `results/final/figures/reliability_{gsm8k,math}.pdf`.

* **The vote-only belief is well calibrated on held-out questions:** ECE 0.014 (GSM8K) and 0.010 (MATH) over all

  questions the judge also tends to fail the correct answer (Section 6), so a FAIL is partly "already counted" in
  the vote split.
* At the few DP-visited states with at least one verdict, the posterior is slightly better calibrated than the
  vote-only belief on GSM8K (ECE 0.033 vs 0.052). The Brier scores are essentially identical (0.229 both).

Also, 78% (GSM8K) and 87% (MATH) of judged answers received the same verdict on all 8 calls, and the judge
failed every one of its 8 calls on 48% / 67% of correct answers (`results/final/judge_analysis/consistency.csv`).

**Explanation.**
1. **The 3B judge is barely better than chance where it would matter.** Its balanced accuracy is 0.60 on GSM8K
   and 0.56 on MATH. On the most contested GSM8K third, where the vote is ambiguous, it is 0.56 [0.49, 0.63],
   because it rejects 66% of correct answers there.
2. **In split vote states a verdict hardly moves the belief.** A PASS has LR 1.39 (GSM8K) / 1.57 (MATH), and a
   FAIL has LR 0.88 / 0.91. On GSM8K a split leader's 0.42 chance of being right becomes 0.50 after a PASS and
   0.39 after a FAIL. Both are still below the λ = 2 submit threshold of 0.67, so the verdict rarely changes the
   decision.
3. **One more sample is more informative and cheaper.** In split states it carries 0.089 / 0.042 bits about
   correctness, versus 0.007 / 0.004 bits for a verdict, about 10× more, at half the cost when c_j = 2·c_s.
4. **Repeating the judge adds almost nothing.** Verdicts on one answer are nearly deterministic, and the
   Beta-Binomial model correctly discounts repeats.

The DP therefore almost never judges, and when it does, the effect on reward is within ±0.03 (Section 4). The
judge is most informative where it is least needed: in unanimous states (PASS LR 2.55 / 3.99), the leader is
already right 98% / 88% of the time.

(−1.968 vs −2.080 over 100 episodes) comes from stopping early and from abstaining in the few episodes where the
first samples happen to split. This is exactly the failure mode that the per-question pass@1 cannot reveal.

## 8. Limitations

* **Few questions.** Only 127 (GSM8K) and 128 (MATH) questions. The per-question SEs are large, and differences
  of a few hundredths of reward need the paired tests above to be seen at all. Results come from one fixed fold
  split; we did not repeat the CV with other splits.
* **State compression.** The DP state (n, a, b, jp, jf) ignores how the remaining n − a − b votes are spread over
  the third and later answers. For example, after n = 8 samples with a 3–2 split, the state cannot tell whether
  the other 3 samples went to one rival answer (2 votes) plus one stray, or to 3 distinct answers. This matters
  most on MATH, where answers are spread over many clusters. Backward induction is exact only for this
  compressed model.
* **Judge independence.** The posterior multiplies the vote-based belief by a judge likelihood, assuming verdicts
  are independent of the votes given correctness. The calibration results (Section 5) suggest this is violated:
  calibration gets slightly worse as verdicts accumulate.
* **Judge coverage.** Verdicts exist only for each question's top-3 answers. Rarer answers use pooled rates, and
  every (question, answer) pass rate is estimated from 8 verdicts.
* **Asymmetric clustering.** Correct samples are grouped by the dataset's correctness labels (an equivalence
  check), while wrong samples are grouped by normalized answer strings. Two wrong answers that are mathematically
  equal but written differently (common on MATH) are split into separate clusters. This makes wrong answers look
  less concentrated than they are and may favor majority voting slightly.
* **Simulation, not live sampling.** Episodes replay stored samples. Real deployment would also have to estimate
  the belief model online.
* **Stronger judge not yet run** (see the TODO below), so "the judge does not help" is established only for the 3B judge.

## 9. Summary

* **Fair tuning narrows the gap.**
  * On GSM8K the DP ties the best train-tuned threshold rule (margin stop with abstain) at λ ≤ 2 and is
    significantly better only at λ = 5 (+0.023 [+0.001, +0.044]).
  * Without abstaining, a tuned margin rule is as good as or slightly better than the DP on both datasets.
  * The DP beats tuned fixed-k voting by about 0.03–0.08 reward per question. This is significant on both tests
    in every run except MATH λ = 2, where only the mean CI excludes 0 (+0.046 [+0.020, +0.075]).
    Example: +0.050 [+0.029, +0.074] on GSM8K at λ = 2.
* **On MATH, the DP with abstaining is significantly better than the tuned abstaining rule at all three penalties.**
  The margin is small (+0.016 to +0.032 reward per question) and comes at equal compute, not at half the compute
  claimed before.
* **Abstaining is the big lever.** On MATH it is worth about +1 reward per question at λ = 2. On GSM8K it helps
  only on average, through a minority of hard questions.
* **The 3B judge adds nothing measurable** at any judge cost (0.5, 1, 2 × c_s). In ambiguous vote states, one
  verdict carries about a tenth of the information of one more sample.
* **The vote-only belief is well calibrated out of sample.** ECE is 0.008–0.017 over all trajectory states and
  over the states the DP visits. It is worse (0.04–0.05) only on the few hundred to few thousand states where the
  DP chose to call the judge.
